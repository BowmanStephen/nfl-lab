# Week 6, in plain English

**The one stat you need: EPA (expected points added).** Before every snap, the down, distance and field spot are worth some number of points. EPA is how much one play moved that number. Add up a QB's dropbacks (throws, sacks and scrambles) and you get roughly how many points his passing was worth. Plus is good, minus is bad. College PPA is the same idea.

### What mattered this week
The better offense per snap won 11 of 14 games. Denver, Tennessee and Cleveland won without it, and all three turned the ball over less than the team they beat.
Turnovers were the biggest swing again. Teams that turned it over less went 8–2, and the Giants' four turnovers cost about 19 points in a seven-point loss.
Field goals decided four games. Cleveland, Arizona and Houston won on kicks with three seconds or less left, and Denver won on a 53-yarder in overtime.

---

### Broncos 17, Seahawks 14 (overtime)
Denver was the worse offense: only 29% of its plays were successes, and it had one big play all night. Seattle ran 80 plays to Denver's 56 but gave the ball away three times: a Rashid Shaheed fumble in Denver territory, a Sam Darnold interception by Kris Abrams-Draine with 1:31 left and Seattle down 14–11, and another by Ja'Quan McMillian in overtime that set up Wil Lutz's 53-yard winner. Jason Myers had tied it with seven seconds left. Those three turnovers cost Seattle about **11 points** of expected scoring, in a game decided by three.

### Texans 34, Jaguars 31 (London)
Both offenses were good, and Houston's was the week's best per snap, adding about **0.22 points a play**. C.J. Stroud wasn't sacked once and his passing was worth about +13 on 35 dropbacks; Trevor Lawrence's was worth about +12 on 43. Travis Hunter caught a touchdown and then intercepted Stroud in the end zone with Houston up 31–28. Cam Little tied it with a 60-yarder with two minutes left, and Ka'imi Fairbairn won it from 49 with three seconds left.

### Bears 31, Falcons 23
Zachariah Branch fumbled twice on returns. He muffed a punt at the Atlanta 10 with Chicago up 14–7, and D'Andre Swift scored three plays later. Then he lost a kickoff return at his own 31, and Cairo Santos's field goal made it 31–7. Kicks and returns swung about **9 points** to Chicago, and Branch's two fumbles were worth about 11 points on their own. Michael Penix Jr. was sacked five times and his passing cost about 13 points; Atlanta's 16 fourth-quarter points, on two Kyle Pitts touchdowns, came down 24.

### Browns 13, Ravens 10
Baltimore led 10–0 at halftime and threw the ball fine: Tyler Huntley's passing was worth about +5, Deshaun Watson's about −8. The Ravens' running game is what lost it. Derrick Henry carried 17 times for 58 yards and fumbled at his own 33 with Baltimore up 10–3, and Cleveland turned it into Audric Estimé's tying touchdown. Rushing EPA adds up what every run was worth, and Baltimore's 19 runs for 59 yards, fumble included, cost about **11 points**. Andre Szmyt missed from 48 with 3:44 left, then won it from 37 on the last play.

### Titans 26, Colts 20
Indianapolis ran 77 plays to Tennessee's 44 and led 13–0 at halftime. But Daniel Jones threw interceptions at the Tennessee 6 and 11, Jonathan Taylor fumbled at his own 33, and those three turnovers cost the Colts about **14 points**. Tennessee's kicking did the rest: Julius Chestnut blocked a punt that Oluwafemi Oladejo returned for a touchdown, and Joey Slye made four field goals, the last two from 49 and 44 after Indianapolis had tied it at 20.

### Patriots 26, Jets 19
The Jets actually kept more plays on schedule, 49% to 43%, but they reached the red zone three times and scored **zero** touchdowns there. A red-zone trip is a drive that gets to the opponent's 20, and settling for a field goal there leaves four points behind. Jason Sanders kicked four field goals, and the Jets' only touchdown was a 48-yard pass to Omar Cooper Jr. Drake Maye's passing was worth about +14 on 35 dropbacks, with touchdowns to Romeo Doubs (42 yards), DeMario Douglas and Hunter Henry. It's the best quarterback game of the week for the second week running.

### Saints 34, Giants 27
The Giants led 10–0 and then gave the ball away four times. Charlie Jones fumbled a kickoff at the Giants' 28 late in the first half, and Tyler Shough hit Bryce Lance on fourth down from the 2 with five seconds left to tie it. Jameis Winston threw two interceptions in the second half, and New Orleans scored 34 straight points. Those four turnovers cost the Giants about **19 points**, the most of any team this week. J.J. McCarthy came in down 34–10, the Giants scored 17 straight, and his fourth-and-1 pass from the Saints' 14 fell incomplete with 12 seconds left.

### Panthers 25, Eagles 14
Philadelphia had **no explosive plays**: not one run of 10 yards or pass of 20. Without big plays, an offense has to string together long drives, and Tanner McKee's 48 dropbacks cost about 9 points. Carolina had seven explosive plays, Chuba Hubbard ran 28 times for 120 yards, and Tommy Tremble caught two touchdowns. The Eagles ran 13 times for 35 yards, after 17 for 20 a week ago.

### Steelers 26, Buccaneers 19 (overtime)
Neither quarterback played well. Both were sacked five times, Aaron Rodgers's passing cost about 14 points, and Jalon Daniels's cost about **17 points** on 52 dropbacks, the worst quarterback game of the week. Last week his passing was worth about +7. Tampa Bay led 16–3 going into the fourth quarter. Then Daniels threw two interceptions, the second returned by Jamel Dean to the Tampa Bay 3 with the game tied, and Chris Boswell's 20-yarder put Pittsburgh ahead. Chase McLaughlin tied it from 53, and Rodgers won it in overtime with a 6-yard touchdown to Germie Bernard.

### Cardinals 18, Rams 16
Arizona never scored a touchdown. It went 0 for 5 in the red zone and won because Chad Ryland made **all six** of his field goals. The last was from 43 yards with three seconds left, after Matthew Stafford's touchdown pass to Colby Parkinson and the extra point put the Rams up 16–15. The Cardinals were the steadier offense: they ran 70 plays to 56, kept 49% of them on schedule and weren't sacked once.
**Lesson:** a kicker is worth more than his kicks. Blake Grupe got hurt covering a kickoff in the first quarter, and punter Ethan Evans kicked the rest of the way. The Rams went for two after their first touchdown, went for it on fourth-and-1 at the Arizona 27, and got nothing either time. They lost by two.

### Chiefs 22, Chargers 6
The Chargers had the week's worst offense by both measures. They lost about 0.31 points a snap, and only **27%** of their plays were successes, meaning only about one in four left them better off than before the snap. Justin Herbert's passing cost about 13 points. Even a 57-yard catch by Quentin Johnston to the Kansas City 1 ended in a field goal. Kenneth Walker III ran 19 times for 128 yards and both Chiefs touchdowns.

### Bills 33, Raiders 30
Both quarterbacks played well: Josh Allen's passing was worth about +10, Kirk Cousins's about +12. Buffalo reached the red zone six times and scored three touchdowns, Tyler Bass made all four of his field goals, and the Bills never turned it over. The game's only turnover was a Cousins interception by Dorian Williams at the Las Vegas 32 with the Raiders down 16–13, and Buffalo scored on the short field that followed, a 12-yard touchdown to Keon Coleman. That one throw cost about **5 points** in a game decided by three.

### Packers 23, Cowboys 13
Dak Prescott threw two first-half interceptions on passes for CeeDee Lamb, one picked off by Evan Williams at the Green Bay 3 and one by Brandon Cisse at the 18. The second set up Jordan Love's 1-yard touchdown run and a 17–0 halftime lead. Those two throws cost Dallas about 8 points, and Prescott's other 54 dropbacks added up to about **zero**. Green Bay converted 11 of 18 third downs.

### 49ers 23, Commanders 17
San Francisco ran 26 times for 102 yards, and those runs added about **+9 points**. That was the best running game of the week. Most runs lose a little expected value, and 20 of 28 offenses' runs lost points this week. Christian McCaffrey scored twice from a yard out, and the 49ers led 23–3 in the third quarter. Washington scored two touchdowns to get within six, but Jayden Daniels's fourth-and-3 pass from the San Francisco 17 fell incomplete with two minutes left.
